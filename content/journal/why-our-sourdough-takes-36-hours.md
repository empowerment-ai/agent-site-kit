People ask us this every week, usually while they're waiting for the Friday juniper loaf. Here's the short version.

## Day one: the starter and the mix

Our starter is flour and water that's been fed daily for years. It's full of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria. In the morning we feed it, and by afternoon it's bubbly and ready. We mix it with flour, water, and salt, then fold the dough every half hour for a few hours.

## Overnight: the cold rest

Shaped loaves go into baskets and into the walk-in cooler overnight. Cold slows the yeast down but lets the bacteria keep working. That's where the flavor comes from: the gentle tang, the toasty sweetness, the depth you don't get from a quick loaf.

## Day two: the bake

The loaves go straight from the cooler into a very hot oven with steam. Cold dough holds its shape and blisters beautifully. Forty minutes later we have a dark, crackling crust.

## Why it's worth it

Long-fermented bread tastes better and keeps longer on your counter. It's slow on purpose.
